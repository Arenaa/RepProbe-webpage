RepProbe

Mahsa Khoshnoodi, Sarah Adel Bargal

Georgetown University · GUCV Lab

RepProbe is a 42K-clip benchmark of real and synthetic videos with exactly k ∈ [1, 20] complete repetitions, controlled for position, coverage, and duration. Counting accuracy collapses at high k across video-language models, yet a linear probe on the last-layer residual still recovers the count: the failure is at generation, not in what the model encodes.

Construction

Exact k from rest-pose bounds

Real clips are cut from official RepCount rest-pose timestamps. Consecutive L-marks define one cycle; a k-clip is a contiguous window of k cycles closed on those rest poses. The label is the number of intervals in the cut, so it cannot drift from the source annotation.

Source & bounds

RepCount videos with L1, L2, … rest-pose frames from the official CSVs.

Cycle intervals

Pair consecutive L marks into half-open [start, end). One interval is one repetition.

k-windows

First k, centered k, and last k cycles when the source is long enough.

Prompt

The cut clip and “return only an integer” are the two inputs to a video-language model.

Because k comes from the annotation, it needs no human or model recount, and a k-clip cannot bleed into the next motion.

Real split

Windows on one source

Four sit-up cycles from one RepCount video. Adjacent cycles share a rest pose. The k-clip for each k is the first k of those intervals; the cut never falls inside a repetition.

Synthetic split

Ten families, duration held fixed

RepProbe-Synthetic contains 10,000 clips (10 families × 20 values of k × 50), each 64 frames and 4 seconds. Each family varies one attribute periodically. Several families are appearance changes rather than motion, so the count has no fitness prior and no length cue. One k = 4 clip from each family:

Flash · k = 4
Textured flash · k = 4
Bounce · k = 4
Pendulum · k = 4
Scale · k = 4
Color change · k = 4
Multi object · k = 4
Shape change · k = 4
Crossing · k = 4
Orbit · k = 4

Duration does not grow with k. A twenty-rep orbit is the same 4 s as a one-rep flash, so a model cannot read the count from clip length.

Diagnosis

Representation vs. output: decodable but poorly reported

On a balanced evaluation set the same Cosmos-Reason2-8B model is queried twice: the integer generated from a count-only prompt, and a linear probe on the last prompt token at the final layer. The comparison localizes error to readout versus representation. Ground truth is the number of rest-pose intervals in the cut.

Main result

High-k collapse is a readout failure

Fine-tuning is not required to observe the split. On the same balanced clips, both readouts agree up to k ≈ 10. Past k ≈ 11, Cosmos-Reason2-8B’s generated error more than doubles while a linear probe on its last-layer residual stays approximately flat.

At k = 14–20, generation MAE is 6.34; the probe on the vector the language head reads is 2.75. Generation is biased low: 38.3% of its answers are 10 or less at k ≥ 14, against 10.6% for the probe. Both gaps hold under bootstrap confidence intervals.
